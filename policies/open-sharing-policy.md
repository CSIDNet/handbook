### &#x20;**1. Purpose**

The Climate Sensitive Infectious Diseases Network is committed to building a global, cooperative community that shares knowledge openly to advance collective learning, climate-informed public health, and equitable access to science. This policy establishes guidelines for open sharing of outputs created by working groups, committees, fellows, and other contributors funded through CSIDNet programs, including participatory budgeting processes.

### **2. Policy Statement**

CSIDNet requires that outputs produced with CSIDNet resources (financial, technical, or community support) be openly shared and made accessible within and beyond the Network.\
\
CSDINet requires that a clear license is selected and justified by providing a brief explanation in the output metadata. CSIDNet strongly encourages open licenses such as:

* **General resources** (documentation, training materials, visuals, and other content): [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).&#x20;
* **Data**: [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) where appropriate. Do not open personal or sensitive data without proper legal and ethical safeguards.
* **Software and code**: [MIT Licenses](https://opensource.org/license/mit), or other permissive software licenses.

<sub>We advise against copyleft licenses, such as CC-BY-SA or GNU GPL, as they can introduce barriers to knowledge sharing and reuse, especially when trying to integrate content or code with more permissive licensing. For example, consider an open access textbook that wants to reuse a figure licensed under CC-BY-SA, but the rest of the book is CC-BY; this incompatibility can create uncertainty, requiring authors to either change the entire licensing approach (unlikely) or omit the figure altogether. For software, imagine a government agency may want to integrate GPL-licensed code into an Early Warning System, but depending on their mandate, they may be required to share their work publicly, or keep it closed for security reasons; understanding whether these cases are compatible with copyleft licenses can be complex and discouraging. There are other ambiguous cases, like whether using copyleft-licensed material in a non-open access journal counts as a commercial use. Rather than relying on strict licensing requirements, we believe that openness and reuse are often better supported through community norms and shared expectations, as reflected in this policy.</sub>



* Where necessary, alternative licenses (e.g., [CC BY-NC](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/),) may be used to balance openness with contextual needs, ethical and legal considerations (“as open as possible, as closed as necessary”).

CSIDNet does not require or encourage authors to pay Author Processing Charges (APC) in order to comply with this policy. Rather, we strongly favor zero-cost or subsidized open routes and encourage the support of scholar-run and scholar-owned journals rather than commercial publishers ([Butler et al. 2023](https://zenodo.org/records/8322555)). We encourage support of [Diamond Open Access](https://www.unesco.org/en/diamond-open-access) journals above other variations of Open Access.

All funded working groups and contributors must commit to this principle at the time of application, during the execution of their engagement, and after its completion.

### **3. Scope of Policy**

This policy applies to all:

* Working groups funded through CSIDNet participatory budgeting.
* Committees and fellows producing outputs with CSIDNet resources.
* Consultants or contractors where open sharing terms are defined in contract clauses.

Covered outputs include, but are not limited to: reports, data sets, software, training materials, presentations, publications, blogs, multimedia, and strategy documents.

### **4. Depositing Outputs**

Recognizing that licensing alone does not ensure access, CSIDNet recommends outputs to be deposited in open repositories appropriate to the output type. For example:

* **General resources** (documentation, training materials, visuals, and other content): Zenodo, Figshare, OSF.&#x20;
* **Papers and preprints**: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/), [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/), [Wellcome Trust](https://wellcomeopenresearch.org/); institutional repositories.&#x20;
* **Data**: Zenodo, Figshare, OSG, institutional repositories.
* **Software**: GitHub, GitLab, and archived versions in Zenodo.

### **5. Authorship and Attribution**

* Outputs should credit CSIDNet where appropriate and acknowledge the role of the participatory budget or other funding source.
* Authors must credit contributors appropriately, including contributor roles when feasible.
* Contributors retain authorship and moral rights.

### **6. Compliance and Monitoring**

* Project, fellowship, and any funding proposals should include a statement of open licensing commitment. For example: “This project commits to releasing all outputs under open licenses consistent with CSIDNet’s Open Sharing Policy.”
* CSIDNet may review outputs for compliance with this policy before dissemination.
* Our intent is collective learning and benefit. Repeated non-compliance without documented rationale  may affect eligibility for future funding or continued participation in CSIDNet activities. When concerns arise, we aim for constructive remediation plans before considering eligibility impacts

### **7. Support and Resources**

CSIDNet will provide:

* Help Desk for open sharing practices ([operations@csidnet.org](mailto:operations@csidnet.org))&#x20;
* Training on open sharing practices
* Sample language and checklists on open sharing practices

### **8. Amendments**

CSIDNet will review this policy annually and possibly amend to reflect evolving best practices in open science, community governance, and nonprofit accountability. Members will be notified of significant updates.
