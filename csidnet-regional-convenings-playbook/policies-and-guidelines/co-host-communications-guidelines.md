# Co-host Communications Guidelines

## How to use this document

This document explains how regional co-hosts represent CSIDNet in communications around the 2026 Regional Convenings. It covers naming, logo usage, tone, credit lines, and the rules that apply to each channel you use: participant emails, social media, event materials, and press or blog posts.

It complements two existing documents that you should keep open alongside this one:

* [**CSID Network Identity Guidelines (October 2025)**](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1pMOXK5i-Fd2yH64f6jBKs3f0SZ8e4MgM/view?usp=drive_link): the master visual identity reference, with full logo, color, and typography specifications.
* <mark style="color:$warning;">**CSIDNet 2026 Regional Convenings: Co-host Email Templates (link to be provided)**</mark>: ready-to-adapt email templates for outreach, applications, decisions, logistics, and post-event follow-up.

Where this document and the Identity Guidelines disagree on a visual rule, the Identity Guidelines take precedence. Where this document and the Email Templates disagree on wording, the Email Templates take precedence for emails.

#### Audience

Communications leads at the five 2026 co-host organizations: icddr,b (Bangladesh); PAMCA c/o OCEAC (Cameroon); Universidad de los Andes (Colombia); Africa Bioethics Network (Kenya); MORU (Thailand).

#### When in doubt

Send a draft to your CSIDNet Events Committee representative before publishing. The five-minute review usually catches anything that needs adjusting and is faster than a correction afterwards.

### 1. Naming CSIDNet

CSIDNet has a formal name and a short form.

#### First mention in any piece of writing

Use the full name with the abbreviation in brackets: **Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet)**. After the first mention in the same piece, use **CSIDNet** on its own.

#### Acceptable variants

* **CSID Network**: a synonym for CSIDNet. Use it where it reads better in formal prose, especially in press, blog, or report-style writing. Treat it as interchangeable with CSIDNet, but pick one form per piece and stick with it.
* **CSID (alone)**: only as an adjective ("CSID tools", "the CSID field", "CSID modeling"). Never as the name of the organization.

#### Common errors to avoid

* **CSID Net, CSID-Net, CSIDnet, csidnet**: incorrect spellings. Always CSIDNet (capital C, S, I, D, N).
* **"the CSIDNet"**: do not put "the" in front of the abbreviation CSIDNet. ("CSIDNet is hosting", not "the CSIDNet is hosting".)
* **Full descriptor with "the"**: the article "the" is fine before the full name "Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network" in flowing prose (for example, "members of the Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network"). The article rule applies to the abbreviation only.
* **CSIDN, CSID-N**: not used. The "Net" is part of the name.

#### Possessive form

Write **CSIDNet's** (apostrophe-s), not "CSIDNets" or "CSIDNet'". Example: CSIDNet's 2026 regional program.

#### Translations

CSIDNet is the same name in every language. Do not translate it. The descriptor (Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network) may be translated for first mention if the rest of the piece is in another language. There is a Spanish-language introductory deck (Intro a CSIDNet) you can reuse for reference. \{% hint style="warning" %\}🔗 Link to be provided\{% endhint %\}

#### How to describe the co-hosting relationship

CSIDNet and the co-host organization share equal billing. Use one of these patterns:<br>

* "co-hosted by \[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] and Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet)"
* "\[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] is co-hosting the \[REGION] convening alongside CSIDNet"
* "the \[REGION] regional convening, co-hosted by \[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] and CSIDNet"

Avoid wording that subordinates one party to the other (for example, "CSIDNet, in partnership with \[CO-HOST]" or "\[CO-HOST], on behalf of CSIDNet"). Both names appear in every public reference to the event.

Use only the official CSIDNet logo files. Do not pull logos from screenshots, presentations, or websites; the resolution and proportions will not match the master files.

Use funder acknowledgement sparingly. The current funder's logo (Wellcome Trust) is not part of the standard co-host lockup; it appears only where the Wellcome Trust review process has approved the piece. More information about funder acknowledgement can be found under section 5.

#### Where to find the files

The logo pack can be found here <mark style="color:$warning;">**(Link to be provided).**</mark> SVG (preferred for print and design software) and PNG variants are provided. Use the "with margins" version for any application that places the logo against a colored background or shape.

#### Color options

* **Mix (full color)**: the default logo color across digital. Use across digital channels as the standard CSIDNet mark.
* **Teal (#044953)**: the secondary one-color option. Use where a single color is needed, including print runs without full-color reproduction and one-color partner mailings.
* **Black**: for printed materials where color printing is not available, and for signed letters or partner mailings.
* **White**: for dark or photographic backgrounds. Pair with sufficient contrast.
* **Neutral white (#EFF4E3)**: for use on the brand teal background.

Do not recolor the globe icon to lime, blue, purple, red, or yellow. Those colors appear only on social media avatars (in the background of the round mark) and on collateral backgrounds, never on the globe itself.

#### Co-host lockup: CSIDNet and your organization together

On every co-branded piece, both logos appear on the same page or panel. Treat them with equal visual weight. Two layouts work:<br>

* **Side by side**, separated by a thin vertical rule (1 pt teal) or a generous gap of clear space. Place CSIDNet on the left and the co-host on the right, or follow the ordering used in your Standing Meeting Notes header if your organization has one.
* **Stacked**, with CSIDNet above the co-host logo and the same gap between them as the height of the smaller logo.

Both logos should appear at the same optical size. If your organization's logo is much wider than CSIDNet's, scale to match heights, not widths. Reference the Co-host Logos folder for the upscaled versions of each co-host mark.

#### When CSIDNet and co-host brand colors meet

If a co-host's primary brand color competes with CSIDNet teal (for example, a warm orange, a red, or a saturated complementary color), follow these rules to keep both brands legible together:<br>

* Keep each logo in its own brand color. Do not recolor either brand to harmonize. Either both logos appear in their full-color forms, or both use a single-color treatment (Mix paired with the co-host's primary color, or both in black on a printed mailing).
* Use a neutral background behind the lockup (neutral white #EFF4E3 or plain white). Avoid placing the lockup on either brand's saturated color block, which would tip the visual weight.
* Increase the white space between the two logos rather than introducing a dividing rule in a third color. A generous gap reads as parity; a colored rule reads as one brand introducing the other.
* When a divider is necessary in tight layouts (badges, footers), use a thin teal rule (1 pt #044953) or a neutral gray rule. Never use a third accent color from CSIDNet's palette as the divider, and never use the co-host's accent.

#### Things not to do

* Do not stretch, skew, rotate, or recolor the logo.
* Do not place the logo on a busy photo background without a solid panel behind it.
* Do not crop the lockup or separate the globe from the wordmark in the primary logo.
* Do not add a tagline, drop shadow, outline, or effect to the logo.
* Do not co-lockup CSIDNet with funder logos. Funders are credited in text or in a separate logo strip (see Section 5).

### 3. Color and typography

#### Color palette

Use the brand teal as the lead color and pick one or two accents for any single piece. The full palette is intended for variety across materials, not within a single piece.

| **Color**              | **HEX** | **Role**                                                                                | **Notes**                                                                                    |
| ---------------------- | ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Teal (primary)**     | #044953 | Headers, links, primary text on light backgrounds, default logo color in one-color uses | Use as the first accent on any new piece. Pairs with neutral white text for inverted panels. |
| **Lime**               | #D3F781 | Highlight blocks, calls to action                                                       | Always pair with teal text. Avoid white text on lime: contrast is too low.                   |
| **Blue**               | #46CBDE | Section markers, stats, infographics                                                    | Pairs with teal text on light shades; with neutral white on the full saturation.             |
| **Purple**             | #BBBDF3 | Soft accent, decorative panels                                                          | Use sparingly. Best behind a teal headline.                                                  |
| **Red**                | #FC8170 | Warm accent, photographic overlays                                                      | Not for warnings or errors. CSIDNet red is friendly, not alarming.                           |
| **Yellow**             | #FFB605 | Energy accent, highlights                                                               | Pair with teal text only; never with neutral white.                                          |
| **Neutral background** | #EFF4E3 | Page backgrounds, body slides, light panels                                             | The default background when teal is too heavy.                                               |

Text color rule: always use either teal (#044953) or neutral white (#EFF4E3) for type. Black is acceptable for body copy in print only. Use the lighter background tints (Light, Medium) only when text is 14 pt or larger; for smaller body text, use the Card, Pill, or Dark variants of the palette for accessibility.

#### Typography

* Inter Tight (Bold and Regular): logo, headlines, subheads, large display copy.
* Inter (Regular): body copy, captions, table contents, email signatures.
* Fallback: Arial, in any environment where Inter and Inter Tight are not available (most email clients, some Word installs). Do not substitute Calibri, Times New Roman, or Helvetica.

Where to download Inter and Inter Tight: [fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter) and [fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter+Tight](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter+Tight). Both are free and open source.

#### Hierarchy

Establish hierarchy through size or by bolding the higher of two levels. Do not use ALL CAPS for headers. Italics are reserved for titles of works (reports, papers) and for short emphasis.

### 4. Tone and voice

CSIDNet writes in a voice that is warm, plain, and peer to peer. The same voice runs through emails, social posts, blog mentions, and event materials. Use it as the default and adapt only where the channel demands something more formal (for example, contractual language in visa support letters).

#### Six principles

* **Warm, collaborative, and equity-centered**. Address the reader as a peer, not as an audience.
* **Clear and accessible**. Avoid jargon. When you must use a technical term (CSID, IPF, EOI), explain it on first mention.
* **Democratic, not hierarchical**. Use "we" more than "I". Credit the network, the committees, and the regional community before highlighting any individual.
* **Humble and transparent**. Share what you tried, what worked, and what did not. Do not overclaim.
* **Reflective and experimental**. Communicate the reasoning and trade-offs behind decisions, not only the outcomes.
* **Urgent yet hopeful**. Climate-driven disease risk is serious; CSIDNet's response is practical and forward-looking. Hold both at once.

#### Spelling and grammar

* **Use American English**: organization, program, behavior, prioritize, centered, color, modeler. This is CSIDNet's house style across all communications.
* **Sentence case for headlines and subject lines**, not Title Case. ("Applications open: Bangladesh CSIDNet Regional Convening", not "Applications Open: Bangladesh CSIDNet Regional Convening".)
* Use the Oxford comma where it removes ambiguity.
* Spell out numbers one to nine; use figures for 10 and above. Always use figures for dates, times, currencies, percentages, and participant counts.
* CSIDNet's standard is U.S. date and time format. Event co-hosts are free to adopt the format most appropriate for their local/regional context.

#### Words and phrases CSIDNet uses

* "Member-led network" and "community of practice" rather than "organization" or "association".
* "Convening" rather than "conference", "summit", or "event" when referring to the regional gatherings.
* "Practitioners, researchers, and technologists" as the umbrella description of participants. ("Modelers, software developers, and end users" is a more precise alternative when the audience is technical.)
* "Inclusive Participation Fund (IPF)" is the formal term. "Financial support" is also acceptable in formal communications. Avoid "bursary" or "travel grant". ("Scholarship" is fine in informal speech.)
* "Expression of interest (EOI)" for the application form, not "registration" or "RSVP".

#### Words and phrases to avoid

* "Stakeholders" (replace with "members", "participants", "co-hosts", or the specific group).
* "Beneficiaries" (CSIDNet does not have beneficiaries; it has members and participants).
* "Capacity building" as a standalone term (be specific about what is being built: governance, software stewardship, peer mentorship).
* "Best practices" (use "shared practices", "lessons learned", or "what we have found works"; CSIDNet is reflective and experimental, not prescriptive).
* "Stakeholder engagement", "leverage", "synergies", "deliverables": corporate vocabulary that does not match CSIDNet's peer-to-peer voice.

### 5. Credits and acknowledgements

#### Standard "About CSIDNet" boilerplate

Use this paragraph at the foot of press releases, blog posts, and any external piece that introduces CSIDNet to a new audience. Edit lightly for length, but keep the four elements: what CSIDNet is, who it serves, what it does, and where it came from.

> _Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet) is a global, member-led network that provides shared socio-technical infrastructure for the climate-health field. CSIDNet enables researchers, modelers, software developers, and practitioners to collaborate across disciplines, institutions, and geographies so that CSID tools and knowledge are usable, durable, and locally led. CSIDNet was established in 2024 with funding support from the Wellcome Trust. Learn more at csidnet.org._

A shorter version, for tweets and social posts:

> _CSIDNet is a global, member-led network for the climate-health field. We help researchers, software developers, and practitioners collaborate so CSID tools are usable, durable, and locally led._

#### Funder acknowledgement

CSIDNet's 2026 program is supported by the Wellcome Trust. Use funder acknowledgement sparingly. Include it where the funding context matters (for example, press releases, formal blog posts, donor-facing reports), and skip it on routine event materials, social posts, and internal communications.

* **Long form** (use when funding context matters): "The 2026 Regional Convenings are part of CSIDNet's program, supported by the Wellcome Trust."
* **Short form**: "Supported by the Wellcome Trust." Use only where appropriate; not every piece needs it.

Confirm with your Events Committee representative whether to include the Wellcome Trust logo on a specific piece. The Wellcome logo is not part of the standard co-host lockup; it appears only where the Wellcome Trust review process has approved the piece.

#### Crediting individuals

* **Quote attribution**: "\[Name], \[Role], \[Organization]". For CSIDNet roles, use "\[Name], \[Committee or Working Group], CSIDNet" (for example, "\[Name], Events Committee, CSIDNet").
* **Photo credits**: "Photo: \[Photographer], \[Year]" or "Photo: \[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION]" if taken in-house.
* **Speaker bios**: keep them under 80 words. Lead with current role, then one or two relevant previous credentials, then geographic affiliation.

#### Crediting co-hosts in CSIDNet-led communications

When CSIDNet writes about your convening, the co-host organization appears in the headline (or first paragraph) and in the boilerplate. Conversely, when you write about your convening, CSIDNet appears in the headline (or first paragraph) and in the boilerplate. The default reference order is alphabetical by organization name; deviate only when ordering carries meaning (for example, the host city comes first).

### 6. Channel guidance

#### 6.1 Emails to participants

The guidance in this section is offered as suggestions, not strict rules. Adapt to fit your audience and context.

Use the co-host email templates provided for reference if necessary. The templates cover outreach, applications, decisions, post-decision logistics, registration, and reimbursement.

#### Defaults

* Send from your own organizational email address. Sign the email in your name and your role at your organization.
* First mention of CSIDNet uses the full name with the abbreviation. Subsequent mentions use CSIDNet.
* Reference the co-hosting relationship in the opening paragraph: "\[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] is co-hosting the \[REGION] regional convening alongside CSIDNet."
* Sign-off: "Warm regards" for outreach and acceptances; "Best regards" for general announcements; "Thank you again" for decline letters. Match the existing template defaults.
* Keep CSIDNet copy lines to a minimum. Do not blind-copy CSIDNet on individual participant emails; instead, share aggregate updates with your Events Committee representative.

#### Email signature block

Recommended structure for the email signature on participant emails:

* \[Your name]
* \[Your role], \[Co-host organization]
* \[Email] | \[Phone, optional]
* Co-hosting the 2026 \[Region] Convening with CSIDNet | \[Event blog URL]

#### Subject line conventions

* **Outreach to individuals**: "You're invited: \[REGION] Regional Convening, \[DATE]"
* **Outreach to organizations**: "Invitation for \[ORG] to participate: \[REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening"
* **Application announcements**: "Applications open: \[REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening, \[DATE]"
* **Reminders**: "\[X] left to apply: \[REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening"
* **Confirmations**: "We received your \[application / expression of interest]: \[REGION] Convening"

Under 60 characters recommended. Front-load the most distinctive word so the line still parses on a phone preview.

### 6.2 Social media (LinkedIn, X, others)

The guidance in this section is offered as suggestions, not strict rules. Adapt to your platform, audience, and campaign.

#### Account handles and tags

* Tag CSIDNet on every post about the convening. The official handles are: LinkedIn @CSIDNet, X @csidnet. (Confirm the exact handles with your Events Committee representative; this list is updated as new accounts are created.)
* Tag the relevant funder when the post mentions funding: @WellcomeTrust on LinkedIn and X.
* Tag your own co-host organization's account, plus any speakers, partners, or attendees mentioned by name (with their consent).

#### Hashtags

* **Primary**: #CSIDNet
* **Event-specific**: #CSIDNet\[Region] (for example, #CSIDNetThailand, #CSIDNetKenya).
* **Topic**: use one or two of #ClimateAndHealth, #InfectiousDisease, #OpenScience, #ClimateHealth. Cap a single post at five hashtags total.

#### Visuals

* **Avatar (round)**: use the CSID Social Logo in the brand color that matches your campaign palette.
* **Banner / cover image**: 1584 x 396 px for LinkedIn, 1500 x 500 px for X. Use the full lockup or the logomark in Mix on neutral, with the convening city and date in Inter Tight Bold.
* **Post images**: square (1080 x 1080) for in-feed images on LinkedIn and X; portrait (1080 x 1350) for Instagram. Keep one logo lockup per image and let the type breathe.
* **Video and Reels**: include a 3-second end card with the CSIDNet logomark and the co-host logo, on a teal or neutral background.

#### Post structure (the default)

* **Hook**: one line, no jargon. ("Applications open for the Bangladesh Convening." rather than "We are pleased to announce that applications are now open...")
* **Body**: two to four short paragraphs. Lead with what is new, then context, then a clear call to action.
* **Call to action**: a single link or instruction. ("Apply by \[DATE]: \[URL]")
* **Mentions and hashtags**: at the end of the post, not interspersed in the body.
* **Image**: square graphic with title, date, and co-host + CSIDNet lockup.

#### Cross-promotion

* When you publish a post about the convening, share it with your Events Committee representative and the CSIDNet Operations Team so the network can amplify it.
* Do not copy and re-post CSIDNet's text verbatim. Add your regional voice and a sentence about why this convening matters from where you sit.

### 6.3 Event materials

#### Cover pages and decks

* Use the CSIDNet presentation template **("Updated Presentation Template (w/o Wellcome logo).pptx" in CSIDNet Presentations)**. Replace the title-slide placeholders with your event name, location, and dates. Add the co-host logo on the title and closing slides.
* Both logos appear on the title slide. CSIDNet's primary logo (Mix / full color) goes top-left or center; the co-host logo follows the lockup rules in Section 2.
* Section divider slides use the brand colors. Pick one accent (lime, blue, purple, red, or yellow) per deck and use it throughout, rather than rotating.

#### Programs and agendas

* **Header**: co-host + CSIDNet lockup at top, on a neutral or white background.
* **Footer**: short credit line ("Co-hosted by \[CO-HOST] and CSIDNet.") in 9 pt Inter Regular, teal. Funder mention is not required on every program or agenda; add it only where the piece sits in a context that calls for it.
* **Body**: Inter Tight Bold for session titles, Inter Regular for descriptions and times. Keep one accent color for time blocks and section markers.

#### Name badges

* **Front**: participant name in Inter Tight Bold, role and organization in Inter Regular below. Logomark (Mix) in the bottom-left corner; co-host logomark or wordmark in the bottom-right.
* **Back (optional)**: wifi password, venue map QR code, emergency contact.

#### Posters and signage

* CSIDNet logo (full lockup, Mix) at top, large enough to read from across the room.
* Co-host logo at the same optical size as CSIDNet, with clear space between.
* Funder credit ("Supported by the Wellcome Trust") is optional and used sparingly. Add it only where the audience or context warrants it.
* Photography or illustration may overlay onto the brand colors. Avoid stock photography that depicts climate or disease in a sensational or scarcity-driven way; CSIDNet imagery favors scientists, communities, and tools at work.

#### Visa support letters and contractual letters

Use the Visa Support Letter template (in \[A]Common-Shared Folder > Templates) and the CSID Letterhead Template. The letterhead already includes the CSIDNet logo and address. Sign on your own organization's headed paper as well, where the receiving consulate or partner expects an institutional letter.

### 6.4 Press, media, and blog

#### Who can speak on behalf of CSIDNet

You speak on behalf of your co-host organization and your role within CSIDNet (for example, as Events Committee co-chair). When asked to speak on behalf of CSIDNet as a whole (its strategy, funding, or governance), refer the request to your Events Committee representative and CSIDNet Operations ([operations@csidnet.org](mailto:operations@csidnet.org)).

Quotes about your convening are yours to give. CSIDNet's central team will not issue conflicting quotes; do let your Events Committee representative know in advance when a press piece is going out.

#### Press releases and media advisories

* **Headline**: lead with the convening, not the funder or the network. ("Bangladesh hosts CSID climate-health convening, \[date]" rather than "Wellcome-funded network gathers in Dhaka").
* **First paragraph**: who, what, where, when, why. Mention CSIDNet by full name on first mention.
* **Boilerplate**: include the standard "About CSIDNet" paragraph from Section 5 at the foot of the release. Add an "About \[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION]" paragraph below it.
* **Contact**: list two media contacts: yours (co-host) and a representative from CSIDNet's shared leadership.

#### Blog posts

* **Author byline**: your name and role at your co-host organization. ("With contributions from \[CSIDNet contributor]" if applicable.)
* **On csidnet.org**: posts use the CSIDNet voice (Section 4) and the standard boilerplate at the foot.
* **On your organization's blog**: write in your organization's voice; reference CSIDNet using the naming rules in Section 1; link to the convening blog post on csidnet.org.

#### Photography and consent

* Get explicit photography consent from every participant at registration. The standard CSIDNet registration form includes the consent question; if your local form does not, add it.
* Do not publish photos of named children, of medical procedures, or of identifiable patients in a clinical setting.
* Caption photos with location, date, and event ("\[Region] CSIDNet Regional Convening, \[Month Year]"). Credit the photographer.

#### Interviews

* CSIDNet's media position is reflective and experimental, not promotional. If you are interviewed, frame the convening as a step in a longer process: what you learned, what you will try next, what is unfinished.
* Decline questions about CSIDNet's funding, future strategy, or internal governance unless your Events Committee representative has briefed you on the answer.
