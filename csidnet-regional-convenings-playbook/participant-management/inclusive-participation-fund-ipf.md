# Inclusive Participation Fund (IPF)

#### What is the IPF?

The CSIDNet Inclusive Participation Fund exists to remove financial barriers that may prevent participants from fully participating in network gatherings, service opportunities, and volunteer initiatives. As a global, volunteer-driven network, CSIDNet is committed to equitable access, shared learning, and broad representation across regions, sectors, and career stages. We recognise that financial constraints can disproportionately affect participation, particularly for members from under-resourced organizations, lower-income contexts, or those with caregiving and accessibility needs.

This fund provides needs-based, flexible support to enable meaningful engagement in both virtual and in-person events. By reducing participation barriers, the fund strengthens the diversity of voices, perspectives, and lived experiences within the network, ensuring that CSIDNet remains inclusive, representative, and mission-aligned. Funding is allocated based on demonstrated need, alignment with network goals, and available resources.

#### What can the IPF cover?

* Full trip sponsorship
* Airfare only
* Accommodation
* Per diem
* Childcare or caregiving support
* Accessibility assistance

#### Who manages the IPF?

{% hint style="warning" %}
The IPF is managed by CSIDNet Finance Committee and operations staff. Co-hosts should not make any promises or commitments to participants about financial support. Direct all participant questions about the IPF to operations@csidnet.org
{% endhint %}

#### Full process schedule by region (2026)

The 2026 IPF process varies by region. Thailand, Bangladesh, and Kenya follow the original 13-step process, in which the Finance Committee (FC) conducts a cost reasonableness review and confirms the shortlist at a standing sync meeting before panel selection. Colombia and Cameroon follow a revised 9-step process (effective June 2026) in which the FC async review and the FC sync meeting are removed; the co-host and EC representative review applications directly, and FC's role is limited to final list approval after panel selection.

#### Review process at a glance

For each region, the review process runs in stages, anchored on each region's deadline:

1. **Staff pre-screen** (week after deadline): eligibility check, budget standardization, need-tier coding using three objective indicators (country income classification, institution type and funding access, individual funding access).
2. **Finance Committee (FC) cost reasonableness review — Thailand, Bangladesh, and Kenya only** (asynchronous): FC members independently review each shortlisted application, recording Advance / Flag / Remove decisions (with required notes for Flag and Remove) against staff-prepared cost benchmarks. The standing biweekly FC meeting after each deadline serves as a synchronous confirmation that the shortlist is ready to hand off to the panel.
   1. June 4 - FC meeting to confirm Thailand shortlist
   2. Jun 18 - FC meeting to confirm Kenya & Bangladesh shortlists
3. **Event-level panel selection** (Meeting 3 - co-host + EC representative): panel reviews the shortlist and selects the final cohort based on relevance, contribution, and cohort balance. For Colombia and Cameroon, the shortlist comes directly from staff pre-screen (no prior FC review).
   1. Suggested dates for Meeting 3 <mark style="color:$primary;">**<- please schedule these at your earliest convenience**</mark>
      1. June 8 - Thailand
      2. June 25 - Jul 2 - Bangladesh
      3. June 25 - July 2 - Kenya
      4. July 9 - Colombia (fixed)
      5. Aug 20 - Aug 27 - Cameroon
4. **FC final approval — Colombia and Cameroon only** (async): after panel selection and the advice process, staff emails the confirmed participant list to all FC members. Three email replies to approve constitutes approval. Any concern is returned to the co-host and EC representative for revision before resubmitting.
5. **Staff validation and notification** (within ~1 week of panel selection and FC approval): confirms selections are within regional allocations; all applicants notified by the regional notification target.

Registration deadlines for selected participants are set per region (typically within 2 weeks after notification). Co-hosts will communicate region-specific registration deadlines after notifications are issued.
