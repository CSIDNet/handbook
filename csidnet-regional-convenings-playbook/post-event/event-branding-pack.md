# Event branding pack

**Co-host Communications Guidelines: How to use the CSIDNet brand in emails, social media, event materials, and press**

Issued May 2026. [Open the original Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1cJVLe1YuVVPqmJcg9gTHqVrDHv3OrQJK/edit).

This document explains how regional co-hosts represent CSIDNet in communications around the 2026 Regional Convenings. It covers naming, logo usage, tone, credit lines, and the rules that apply to each channel.

It complements two existing documents that you should keep open alongside this one:

* CSID Network Identity Guidelines (October 2025): the master visual identity reference, with full logo, color, and typography specifications.
* CSIDNet 2026 Regional Convenings: Co-host Email Templates: ready-to-adapt email templates for outreach, applications, decisions, logistics, and post-event follow-up.

Where this document and the Identity Guidelines disagree on a visual rule, the Identity Guidelines take precedence. Where this document and the Email Templates disagree on wording, the Email Templates take precedence.

**Audience:** Communications leads at the five 2026 co-host organizations: icddr,b (Bangladesh); PAMCA c/o OCEAC (Cameroon); Universidad de los Andes (Colombia); Africa Bioethics Network (Kenya); MORU (Thailand).

**When in doubt:** Send a draft to your CSIDNet Events Committee representative before publishing. The five-minute review usually catches anything that needs adjusting and is faster than a correction afterwards.

## 1. Naming CSIDNet

**First mention in any piece of writing:** Use the full name with the abbreviation in brackets: Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet). After the first mention in the same piece, use CSIDNet on its own.

**Acceptable variants:**

* CSID Network: a synonym for CSIDNet. Use it where it reads better in formal prose, especially in press, blog, or report-style writing. Pick one form per piece.
* CSID (alone): only as an adjective ("CSID tools", "the CSID field", "CSID modeling"). Never as the name of the organization.

**Common errors to avoid:**

* CSID Net, CSID-Net, CSIDnet, csidnet: incorrect spellings. Always CSIDNet (capital C, S, I, D, N).
* "the CSIDNet": do not put "the" in front of the abbreviation. ("CSIDNet is hosting", not "the CSIDNet is hosting".)
* CSIDN, CSID-N: not used.

**Possessive form:** Write CSIDNet's (apostrophe-s). Example: CSIDNet's 2026 regional program.

**Translations:** CSIDNet is the same name in every language. Do not translate it. The descriptor may be translated for first mention if the rest of the piece is in another language.

**Co-hosting relationship:** Use one of these patterns:

* "co-hosted by [CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] and Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet)"
* "[CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] is co-hosting the [REGION] convening alongside CSIDNet"
* "the [REGION] regional convening, co-hosted by [CO-HOST ORGANIZATION] and CSIDNet"

Avoid wording that subordinates one party to the other ("CSIDNet, in partnership with [CO-HOST]" or "[CO-HOST], on behalf of CSIDNet"). Both names appear in every public reference.

## 2. Logos

Use only the official CSIDNet logo files (SVG preferred; PNG also provided). Do not pull logos from screenshots, presentations, or websites. Use the "with margins" version for any application that places the logo against a colored background.

**Logo color variants:**

* Mix (full color): the default across digital channels.
* Teal (#044953): for single-color needs, including print without full-color reproduction.
* Black: for printed materials without color printing, and for signed or partner letters.
* White: for dark or photographic backgrounds.
* Neutral white (#EFF4E3): for use on the brand teal background.

Do not recolor the globe icon to lime, blue, purple, red, or yellow.

**Co-host lockup:** Both logos appear on the same page or panel at equal visual weight.

* Side by side: separated by a thin vertical rule (1 pt teal) or generous clear space. CSIDNet on the left, co-host on the right.
* Stacked: CSIDNet above the co-host logo, with equal gap between them.

Scale to match heights, not widths. If co-host and CSIDNet brand colors compete, use a neutral background (#EFF4E3 or white) behind the lockup and increase white space rather than adding a divider in a third color.

**Things not to do:**

* Do not stretch, skew, rotate, or recolor the logo.
* Do not place the logo on a busy photo without a solid panel behind it.
* Do not separate the globe from the wordmark.
* Do not add taglines, drop shadows, outlines, or effects.
* Do not co-lockup CSIDNet with funder logos. Credit funders in text or a separate logo strip.

## 3. Color and typography

**Colors:** Use teal (#044953) as the lead color and pick one or two accent colors (lime, blue, purple, red, or yellow) per piece. Do not use all accents in a single piece.

* Text color: always teal (#044953) or neutral white (#EFF4E3). Black is acceptable for body copy in print only.
* Light/medium background tints: only when text is 14 pt or larger.

**Fonts:**

* Inter Tight (Bold and Regular): logo, headlines, subheads, large display copy.
* Inter (Regular): body copy, captions, table contents, email signatures.
* Fallback: Arial (for email clients and environments where Inter is not available). Do not substitute Calibri, Times New Roman, or Helvetica.
* Download: fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter and fonts.google.com/specimen/Inter+Tight (free, open source).

**Hierarchy:** Use size or bolding for heading levels. No ALL CAPS headers. Italics reserved for titles of works and short emphasis.

## 4. Tone and voice

CSIDNet writes in a voice that is warm, plain, and peer to peer. Use it as the default and adapt only where the channel requires a different register.

**Six principles:**

1. Warm, collaborative, and equity-centered. Address the reader as a peer, not an audience.
2. Clear and accessible. Avoid jargon. Explain technical terms (CSID, IPF, EOI) on first mention.
3. Democratic, not hierarchical. Use "we" more than "I". Credit the network, committees, and regional community before highlighting individuals.
4. Humble and transparent. Share what you tried, what worked, and what did not. Do not overclaim.
5. Reflective and experimental. Communicate reasoning and trade-offs, not only outcomes.
6. Urgent yet hopeful. Climate-driven disease risk is serious; CSIDNet's response is practical and forward-looking.

**Spelling and grammar:**

* American English: organization, program, behavior, prioritize, centered, color, modeler.
* Sentence case for headlines and subject lines, not Title Case.
* Oxford comma where it removes ambiguity.
* Spell out numbers one to nine; figures for 10 and above. Always figures for dates, times, currencies, percentages, and participant counts.

**Words CSIDNet uses:**

* "Member-led network" and "community of practice" rather than "organization" or "association".
* "Convening" rather than "conference", "summit", or "event" for the regional gatherings.
* "Inclusive Participation Fund (IPF)" as the formal term. "Financial support" also acceptable. Avoid "bursary" or "travel grant".
* "Expression of interest (EOI)" for the application form, not "registration" or "RSVP".

**Words to avoid:**

* "Stakeholders" (use "members", "participants", "co-hosts", or the specific group).
* "Beneficiaries" (CSIDNet has members and participants, not beneficiaries).
* "Capacity building" as a standalone term (be specific: governance, software stewardship, peer mentorship).
* "Best practices" (use "shared practices", "lessons learned", or "what we have found works").
* "Stakeholder engagement", "leverage", "synergies", "deliverables".

## 5. Credits and acknowledgements

**Standard "About CSIDNet" paragraph** (use at the foot of press releases, blog posts, and external pieces introducing CSIDNet):

> Climate Sensitive Infectious Disease Network (CSIDNet) is a global, member-led network that provides shared socio-technical infrastructure for the climate-health field. CSIDNet enables researchers, modelers, software developers, and practitioners to collaborate across disciplines, institutions, and geographies so that CSID tools and knowledge are usable, durable, and locally led. CSIDNet was established in 2024 with funding support from the Wellcome Trust. Learn more at csidnet.org.

**Short version** (tweets and social posts):

> CSIDNet is a global, member-led network for the climate-health field. We help researchers, software developers, and practitioners collaborate so CSID tools are usable, durable, and locally led.

**Funder acknowledgement:**

* Long form: "The 2026 Regional Convenings are part of CSIDNet's program, supported by the Wellcome Trust."
* Short form: "Supported by the Wellcome Trust."
* Confirm with your Events Committee representative before including the Wellcome Trust logo on any piece.

**Quote attribution:** "[Name], [Role], [Organization]". For CSIDNet roles: "[Name], [Committee or Working Group], CSIDNet".

**Photo credits:** "Photo: [Photographer], [Year]" or "Photo: [CO-HOST ORGANIZATION]" if taken in-house.

**Speaker bios:** Keep under 80 words. Lead with current role, then one or two relevant previous credentials, then geographic affiliation.

## 6. Channel guidance

### 6.1 Emails to participants

Send from your own organizational email address, signed in your name and role.

**Defaults:**

* First mention of CSIDNet uses the full name with the abbreviation.
* Reference the co-hosting relationship in the opening paragraph.
* Sign-off: "Warm regards" for outreach and acceptances; "Best regards" for general announcements; "Thank you again" for decline letters.
* Do not BCC CSIDNet on individual participant emails. Share aggregate updates with your Events Committee representative instead.

**Email signature block:**

```
[Your name]
[Your role], [Co-host organization]
[Email] | [Phone, optional]
Co-hosting the 2026 [Region] Convening with CSIDNet | [Event blog URL]
```

**Subject line conventions:**

* Outreach to individuals: "You're invited: [REGION] Regional Convening, [DATE]"
* Outreach to organizations: "Invitation for [ORG] to participate: [REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening"
* Application announcements: "Applications open: [REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening, [DATE]"
* Reminders: "[X] left to apply: [REGION] CSIDNet Regional Convening"
* Confirmations: "We received your [application / expression of interest]: [REGION] Convening"

Keep subject lines under 60 characters. Front-load the most distinctive word.

### 6.2 Social media

Tag CSIDNet on every post: LinkedIn @CSIDNet, X @csidnet.

**Hashtags:**

* Primary: #CSIDNet
* Event-specific: #CSIDNet[Region] (e.g. #CSIDNetThailand, #CSIDNetKenya)
* Topic (pick one or two): #ClimateAndHealth, #InfectiousDisease, #OpenScience, #ClimateHealth
* Maximum 5 hashtags per post.

**Image sizes:**

* Square (1080x1080): in-feed images on LinkedIn and X.
* Portrait (1080x1350): Instagram.
* LinkedIn banner: 1584x396.
* X banner: 1500x500.
* Video end card: 3 seconds, both logos on teal or neutral background.

**Post structure:**

* Hook: one line, no jargon. ("Applications open for the Bangladesh Convening." rather than "We are pleased to announce...")
* Body: 2-4 short paragraphs. Lead with what is new, then context, then call to action.
* Call to action: a single link or instruction.
* Mentions and hashtags at the end, not interspersed in the body.

Do not copy and re-post CSIDNet's text verbatim. Add your regional voice.

### 6.3 Event materials

**Presentations:** Use the CSIDNet presentation template. Both logos appear on the title slide. Pick one accent color per deck and use it throughout.

**Programs and agendas:**

* Header: co-host + CSIDNet lockup at top on a neutral or white background.
* Footer: "Co-hosted by [CO-HOST] and CSIDNet." in 9 pt Inter Regular, teal.
* Session titles in Inter Tight Bold; descriptions and times in Inter Regular.

**Name badges:**

* Front: participant name in Inter Tight Bold, role and organization in Inter Regular below. CSIDNet logomark (Mix) bottom-left; co-host logo bottom-right.
* Back (optional): wifi password, venue map QR code, emergency contact.

**Photography:** Avoid stock photography that depicts climate or disease in a sensational or scarcity-driven way. Favor images of scientists, communities, and tools at work.

**Visa support and contractual letters:** Use the Visa Support Letter template and the CSID Letterhead Template (in [A]Common/Shared Folder > Templates). Sign on your own organization's letterhead or co-sign where required.

### 6.4 Press, media, and blog

**Who can speak for CSIDNet:** You speak on behalf of your co-host organization and your role within CSIDNet (for example, as Events Committee member). When asked to speak on behalf of CSIDNet as a whole, refer to your Events Committee representative.

**Press releases:**

* Headline: lead with the convening, not the funder or the network.
* First paragraph: who, what, where, when, why.
* Boilerplate: include the standard "About CSIDNet" paragraph (Section 5) and an "About [CO-HOST]" paragraph at the foot.
* List two media contacts: yours (co-host) and one from CSIDNet's shared leadership.

**Blog posts:**

* On csidnet.org: use the CSIDNet voice (Section 4) and the standard boilerplate at the foot.
* On your organization's blog: write in your organization's voice; reference CSIDNet using the naming rules in Section 1; link to the convening blog post on csidnet.org.

**Approval flow:**

* Emails using existing templates: no central approval needed.
* Net-new emails or substantial template changes: send to your Events Committee representative.
* Social posts on co-host accounts: no central approval; tag CSIDNet.
* Blog posts and press releases: 3 working days' notice to your Events Committee representative.
* Anything using the Wellcome Trust logo or naming another funder: 5 working days, Events Committee representative and operations@csidnet.org.
* Crisis or sensitive press: contact your Events Committee representative the same day.
