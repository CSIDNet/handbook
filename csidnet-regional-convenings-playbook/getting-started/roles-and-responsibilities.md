# Roles & Responsibilities

#### Coordination Between CSIDNet and Co-Hosts

Each regional convening is planned by a small committee made up of three groups: the co-host team on the ground, Events Committee (EC) representatives who serve as the CSIDNet-side planning partners, and CSIDNet staff who manage coordination and admin. The Finance Committee plays a defined role in the Inclusive Participation Fund (IPF) selection process. Angela Okune leads event design and trains both EC reps and staff so they can support co-hosts consistently across regions.

#### Role: Co-Host Planning Team

The co-host team owns the event on the ground. You are responsible for the local decisions, i.e. venue, logistics, speakers, social programming, local communications, issuing visa support letters, and post-event documentation. You bring regional knowledge and relationships that no one else can provide.

Within your team, designate at minimum: a logistics lead, a programming lead, a documentation lead, and a communications lead. These can be combined across fewer people if needed, but all four functions must be covered.

Your primary planning partners from the CSIDNet side are your EC representatives. For admin and operational questions, your first point of contact is Livini (CSIDNet Staff Coordinator). For questions about program design or alignment with CSIDNet goals, go to your EC reps.

You are contractually required to participate in four planning meetings with your EC representatives, covering: (1) big picture vision, logistics, and venue; (2) program, agenda, and speakers; (3) Inclusive Participation Fund and participant selection; and (4) final logistics and details. Templates for these meetings will be in the shared Google Drive folder created for you by CSIDNet staff. A representative with decision-making authority must attend each meeting. Missing more than two without prior written notice to [operations@csidnet.org](mailto:operations@csidnet.org) voids the subaward agreement.

Your draft event program and speaker list must be submitted to the CSIDNet Events Committee no less than 45 days before the event.

#### Role: Events Committee Representative (1-2 per co-host region)

You are the planning partner between CSIDNet and the co-host team. Your job is collaborative support and quality assurance, not oversight. You bring institutional knowledge, network connections, and consistency of standards so that co-hosts don't have to figure things out from scratch.

In practice, this means:

* Participating in all four required planning meetings with your co-host team, on the topics above
* Reviewing the draft program and speaker list when it is submitted (45 days out), with particular attention to disciplinary balance, geographic diversity, and alignment with CSIDNet goals and sharing with EC and staff for input before giving final approval on behalf of CSIDNet.
* Serving on the Stage 2 event-level selection panel for the Inclusive Participation Fund, jointly with the co-host, to select scholarship recipients from the Finance Committee shortlist
* Escalating anything that the individual EC rep does not know to either CSIDNet staff (relating to ops and admin) or to the full EC (related to programmatic questions).
* Serving as a thought partner, not a gatekeeper

You are not responsible for executing logistics. One EC rep per region must be assigned to the IPF selection panel for that region. EC reps who are also co-hosts for a given region recuse from the IPF scoring for that region.

#### Role: Staff Coordinator (Livini Perera)

You are the operational point of contact and coordinator for all regional convenings on the staff side. Your job is to make sure nothing falls through the cracks administratively, and that co-host teams and EC reps always know where to go.

Concretely, this means:

* Serving as the first point of contact for any admin or operational question from co-host teams or EC reps
* Maintaining and updating the co-host kit in GitBook so it is always current
* Tracking all four required planning meetings across regions, and following up on any missed attendance
* Coordinating with co-hosts on registration and also ensuring they provide visa support letters and other administrative deliverables
* Tracking planning progress and post-event reporting deadlines across all regions via Github, and flagging delays to Angela
* Keeping a clear picture of what is happening across all convenings at all times - you should never be surprised by a deadline
* Attending all EC meetings to get quick updates across all events
* Manage paperwork related to distributing individual scholarships for IPF
* Track payments to co-host organizations and liaise with CS\&S Finance

Event design and program quality are outside this role's current scope. When those questions arise, route them to the EC representative or Angela.

#### Role: Finance Committee

The Finance Committee (FC)'s role in the regional convenings is specific: they conduct cost reasonableness review for Inclusive Participation Fund applications. They do not assess financial need (that is done by staff in pre-screening) and they do not make final selection decisions (that is done by the event-level panel in Stage 2).

Each FC member scores applications independently on a 1-4 scale against a cost benchmark reference document prepared by staff. FC members who are also regional co-hosts recuse from scoring their own region. Staff aggregate the scores and produce a shortlist for each region, which is then passed to the Stage 2 panel.

#### Role: Event Design Advisor (Angela Okune)

Angela runs a train-the-trainers session on May 12th for EC representatives and Livini before regional planning cycles begin. The goal is to give both groups a shared framework for what makes a good convening, so they can support co-hosts consistently without Angela needing to be involved in each region directly.

Angela will support coordinating the IPF application process across all stages: running the pre-screen, preparing the cost benchmark reference document, aggregating Finance Committee scores, and producing regional shortlists.

Angela is not a standing member of any regional planning committee. If significant design questions arise mid-cycle that EC reps cannot resolve, they should flag to Angela directly.

#### Role: Operations Advisor (Ragıp Zık)

Ragıp is the liaison between the CS\&S (the fiscal host), CSIDNet and the regional co-hosts. His goal is to streamline the documentation processes.

Ragıp ensures compliance by communicating the CS\&S financial reporting policies clearly, and follows up with co-hosts to make sure all necessary documents for reporting are collected and submitted.

Ragıp also creates streamlined clear documentation for IPF recipients.

#### Role: Events Committee (EC)

The Events Committee is the primary decision-making body for regional convening planning. Individual EC representatives handle day-to-day collaboration with co-host teams, but the full EC holds collective authority over decisions that affect the integrity, consistency, or direction of the regional events program.

The EC meets at regular intervals to receive updates from all EC representatives, surface issues that may be common across regions, and make decisions that individual reps should not navigate alone.

**Decision-making mandate**

The EC makes or ratifies decisions when:

* An individual EC rep is uncertain how to handle a situation, whether or not it is likely to affect other regions
* A question or conflict has implications that extend across multiple regional convenings
* A co-host team and their EC reps reach an impasse that requires a broader mandate to resolve
* A decision involves interpreting or applying CSIDNet standards in a new way

When in doubt, EC reps should bring questions to the full EC rather than decide unilaterally. The cost of over-escalating is low; the cost of inconsistent decisions across regions is high.

**Draft program review**

When a co-host team submits their draft event program and speaker list (45 days before the event), EC reps bring it to the full EC and staff for a collective review window. The EC and staff may provide input, recommendations, and suggestions. Final approval authority, however, sits with the regional planning committee -- the co-host team and their EC reps -- who take the EC's input into account and make the final call.

**Relationship to other roles**

Angela Okune serves in an advisory capacity to the EC on event design and program quality. She is not a standing member of regional planning committees and does not hold final authority over convening decisions. The EC is the ultimate authority body for regional event planning. Angela should be consulted when design questions arise that fall outside the EC's expertise or established frameworks, but the EC is not required to adopt her recommendations.

#### Decision-Making Matrix

**Glossary**

* Decides: holds final authority; this role's sign-off closes the matter.
* Leads: owns the work and drives it forward; does not have final authority alone.
* Reviews: receives the work and provides input within a defined window; not a veto.
* Executes: carries out the action once a decision is made; no authority over the decision itself.
* Tracks: monitors progress and flags delays or missed deadlines; no authority over content or outcome.
* Notified: informed after the fact; no role in the decision.

#### When to escalate to the full Events Committee

The guiding principle: if an EC rep is uncertain, they escalate. The cost of over-escalating is low; the cost of inconsistent decisions across regions is high. Below are concrete scenarios to calibrate when escalation is warranted.

Scenario 1: A co-host wants to add a speaker who raises concerns. An EC rep reviews the draft speaker list and notices a proposed speaker has publicly criticized CSIDNet or holds positions that conflict with the network's values. The EC rep is unsure whether this rises to the level of a programming conflict or is just a difference of opinion. This is an escalation situation: the EC rep should not decide alone, because the standard applied here will implicitly set precedent for other regions.

**Scenario 2: A co-host misses a required planning meeting:** The subaward agreement specifies that missing more than two meetings without prior written notice voids the agreement. A co-host misses one meeting with informal notice. The EC rep is unsure whether informal notice counts. Rather than interpret the contract language unilaterally, this goes to the EC and likely to staff and Ragip for contract implications.

**Scenario 3: A co-host requests a program format change:** A co-host team proposes a format significantly different from the regional convening model, for example, replacing plenary sessions with a fully unconference-style agenda. The EC reps for that region are supportive, but the change is substantial enough to affect the overall coherence of the regional events program. This is an EC-level decision, not a regional planning committee decision.

**Scenario 4: An IPF applicant disputes the tiering outcome:** A participant who was pre-screened into a lower financial need tier contacts the co-host to challenge the outcome. The co-host escalates to their EC reps, who are unsure how to handle an appeal process that doesn't yet formally exist. This goes to the full EC, who can set a consistent policy across regions and loop in Angela and Ragip as appropriate.

**Scenario 5: A situation is unique to one region but still unclear:** One region's co-host is in a country where the recommended visa support letter format is not accepted. This may not affect other regions, but the EC rep doesn't know what authority they have to deviate from the standard template. They escalate to the full EC anyway, unique situations that require interpreting or adapting CSIDNet standards belong at the EC level.
