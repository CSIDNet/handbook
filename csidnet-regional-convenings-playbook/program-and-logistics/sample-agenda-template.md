# Sample Agenda Template

These agenda templates offer illustrative structures that co‑hosts can adapt for their regional CSIDNet convenings. The templates emphasize collaboration, capacity strengthening, scientific exchange, local stakeholder engagement and climate‑sensitive infectious disease themes such as modelling, forecasting and open‑source tools￼. Each agenda builds in interactive sessions so that participants meet peers, learn from hands-on demonstrations, and move beyond passive listening. Co‑hosts should customise the activities to suit their local context and goals using the “[Worksheet for Aligning Event Goals with Activities](https://www.codeforsociety.org/eventfund/resources/worksheet-for-aligning-event-goals-with-activities)” to link goals to appropriate activities (e.g., connecting people, transferring information, training skills, creating supportive environments, decision‑making, doing things together and celebrating).

Optional add-ons/adaptation tips

* Field or site visit: If logistically feasible, a two‑day agenda allows for a field visit to a local laboratory, community project or relevant climate‑impacted area. Replace or combine one of the midday sessions.
* Evening social activity: Plan a cultural excursion or community dinner to build trust and highlight regional unique aspects.
* Resource mapping: Use a visual format (e.g., sticky‑note wall or digital board) to map resources and commitments, making it easier to identify collaboration opportunities.

### Sample 1‑Day Agenda (8 hours)

08:30‑09:00 – Arrival & registration: informal coffee/tea and check‑in.

09:00‑09:30 – Welcome & purpose: introduce CSIDNet, regional objectives and the day’s goals.

09:30‑10:15 – Icebreaker & small‑group introductions: facilitated exercise to help participants get acquainted.

10:15‑11:15 – Lightning talks & tool demos: 5–10 minute presentations of local projects with time for questions.

11:15‑11:30 – Break: refreshments and informal discussion.

11:30‑12:45 – Interactive workshop: hands‑on session exploring a tool, dataset or method.

12:45‑13:45 – Lunch: encourage seating by interest area or random assignment to mix participants.

13:45‑14:30 – Panel & fishbowl conversation: moderated discussion with diverse panellists; audience members can join via an open seat.

14:30‑15:30 – Breakout sessions: parallel small‑group discussions or unconference topics proposed by participants.

15:30‑16:00 – Break: light snacks.

16:00‑17:00 – Report‑back & synthesis: groups share insights; facilitators highlight common themes and next steps.

17:00‑17:30 – Closing & reflection: collect feedback and summarise the day’s achievements.



### Sample 1.5‑Day Agenda (12 hours)

Day 1 (evening)

16:00‑16:30 – Arrival & networking: registration and mingling.

16:30‑17:00 – Welcome & orientation: introduce CSIDNet and the convening goals.

17:00‑18:00 – Community storytelling session: brief stories from local practitioners highlighting regional challenges.

18:00‑19:00 – Interactive poster & demo hour: stations for tool demos, posters and mini‑activities.

19:00‑19:30 – Dinner & informal networking.

19:30‑20:00 – Evening wrap‑up: participants share one thing they learned and one question for the next day.<br>

Day 2

09:00‑09:15 – Morning welcome & recap.

09:15‑10:15 – Keynote & Q\&A: thought‑provoking talk that invites discussion.

10:15‑11:30 – Parallel workshops: concurrent skill‑building sessions (e.g., building an early‑warning dashboard, data governance).

11:30‑11:45 – Break.

11:45‑12:45 – Open space/unconference: participants propose topics and form discussion circles.

12:45‑13:45 – Lunch.

13:45‑14:45 – Fishbowl debate: moderated debate on a timely question; participants rotate into the discussion.

14:45‑15:00 – Break.

15:00‑16:00 – Collaborative action planning: groups identify opportunities for collaboration and develop action plans.

16:00‑16:30 – Closing & next steps: share action plans, summarise commitments and outline follow‑up.

### Sample 2 ‑ Day Agenda (16 hours)

Day 1

09:00‑09:30 – Arrival & registration.

09:30‑10:00 – Welcome & regional framing: introduce CSIDNet, goals and local context.

10:00‑11:00 – Plenary: state of the science & practice, featuring multiple speakers with diverse perspectives.

11:00‑11:15 – Break.

11:15‑12:30 – Interactive mini‑conference: participants rotate through short stations for tool demos, skills clinics or problem clinics.

12:30‑13:30 – Lunch with topic‑labelled tables to prompt thematic discussions.

13:30‑15:00 – Participatory design lab: mixed groups co‑create solutions to a regional problem.

15:00‑15:15 – Break.

15:15‑16:30 – Panel & fishbowl conversation on a regional theme.

16:30‑17:00 – Reflection circle: share highlights and questions from Day 1.

19:00‑21:00 – Optional social activity: community dinner or cultural event.

Day 2

09:00‑09:15 – Morning check‑in.

09:15‑10:45 – Deep‑dive workshops: longer interactive sessions on topics like advanced modelling or participatory monitoring.

10:45‑11:00 – Break.

12:30‑13:30 – Lunch.

Using the Worksheet to Connect Goals and Activities

Before finalising an agenda, co‑hosts should complete the [Worksheet for Aligning Event Goals with Activities￼](https://zenodo.org/record/6829247). The worksheet helps organisers articulate:

* Event purpose and message: A concise statement (≤7 words) describing what you want participants to take away.
* Participant knowledge: What attendees already know and where gaps exist.
* Why participants will attend: Clarifying motivations helps tailor content and incentives.
* Goal–activity matching: The worksheet lists goal types such as creating connections between people, transferring information, training participants, creating a specific environment, making decisions, doing something together and celebrating￼.  For each goal, organisers brainstorm activities (e.g., icebreakers, workshops, decision‑making exercises) that support it.
