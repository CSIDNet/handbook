# In-Person Event Participation Guidelines

CSID Network is committed to creating and maintaining an environment where everyone is welcomed, respected, and supported. We want everyone to have an opportunity to participate and contribute, and to be valued for their skills, experience, and unique perspectives.

Our gatherings are diverse, by any measure. Participants have arrived, representing a broad range of experiences and viewpoints. Apart from learning and sharing, much of what we are trying to accomplish at these events involves building new relationships and establishing support that will bind us all together beyond this gathering.

While we do not want to dictate behavior or limit expression in any way, we provide the following guidelines as a starting point for collaboration and community building during gatherings. We will further deepen these guidelines by setting community agreements at the start of our in-person gathering together.

We ask all CSIDNet participants to review the following Participation Guidelines:

**Help us to realize the full potential of this gathering**\
Our goal is to share lessons about how to develop CSID models and software tools that build capacity within communities to respond to CSIDs. Our belief is that relationship building and dialogue are fundamental parts of realizing this goal. As you share and learn from others, understand that we are also trying to discern what works and what doesn’t work in peer-to-peer skill and knowledge transfer. If we each manage to both teach and learn, our time at this gathering will have been well spent.

**Take advantage of in-person time**

Remember that in-person gatherings are an opportunity to connect, learn, and collaborate for the impactful and relevant CSIDNet initiatives from global to the community level. We will have Internet access for the event, but we request everyone to take as much advantage of the opportunity to connect face-to-face with the people around you, and disconnect from your electronics. We realize it may be exhausting to constantly be engaging with others In Real Life (IRL), so when your social battery is used up, you are welcome to find a quiet space to recharge and rejoin the group when you are able to.

**Foster a culture of mutual respect for others at all times**

Effective sharing and learning can only take place when interactions are built on a foundation of respect for others. It will never be acceptable to insult others or demonstrate disrespect, even in moments of philosophical disagreement and passionate exchange. Please also be considerate in your actions: refrain from side conversations while others are speaking, refrain from using cellphones and computers (when not part of sessions), and honor the posted schedule by arriving promptly for sessions so that the time of others is respected. The gathering brings together people from diverse cultural backgrounds; therefore, we ask all participants to embody the spirit of respectful intercultural communication and interactions throughout the meeting period.

**Include everybody in the circle**

We envision this gathering not as a forum to showcase expertise but rather as a collaborative space where we can collectively enhance our understanding, acquire new perspectives, exchange valuable insights, and strengthen our professional connections. We should strive to ensure that no one is left out. Aim to talk to everyone as often as possible and speak in a language that is accessible to all. In particular, please define acronyms and technical jargon when using them in discussion.

**Embrace a spirit of sharing**

We believe that everyone at this gathering is an expert in their own realm, and encourage each participant to please contribute their wisdom to the mix. All of us know something and none of us knows everything, that’s why we are in a community.

**Ask questions early and often**

Participants are warmly invited to ask questions; there is no such thing as a “stupid question”. Before asking your question, let the speaker end their statement instead of interrupting the idea being shared. Listen to the questions that others ask so that you do not repeat someone else’s question. If you don’t understand what someone has shared, do ask for further clarification.

**Share first, debate later**

Many among us possess strong beliefs on matters of technology, politics, philosophy, and community. While we endorse debate as a key component in building understanding, we ask that participants focus on finding common ground for sharing during sessions, and endeavor to debate “offline”, outside of sessions, with others who welcome the debate.

**Respect the security and safety of others**

We understand that, as participants of these events, most of us will wish to document for both personal and professional reasons. But consider that some participants may not wish to have photographs or other information about them posted on the Internet. So please ask for consent before sharing someone’s names, photographs/videos, and other information. Please respect those who wish to remain anonymous, and don't take or tag photos of participants who do not want their photos and information published.

**We do not tolerate harassment of participants in any form**\
The gathering is dedicated to providing a safe space and a harassment-free experience for everyone. Attendees are expected to treat all people and facilities with respect and help create a welcoming environment. If you notice behavior that fails to meet this standard, please speak up and help keep the gathering as respectful as possible.

Harassment includes offensive verbal comments related to gender, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, body size, race, religion, or other protected class status, sexual images in public spaces, deliberate intimidation, stalking, following, harassing photography or recording, sustained disruption of talks/workshops or other events, inappropriate physical contact, and unwelcome sexual attention.

Participants who are asked to stop any harassing behavior are expected to comply immediately.

If a participant engages in harassing behavior, the organizers may take any action they deem appropriate, including warning the offender or expulsion from the gathering.&#x20;

If you are being harassed, notice that someone else is being harassed, or have any other concerns, please contact a member of the CSIDNet staff immediately or email [operations@csidnet.org](mailto:operations@csidnet.org).

In sum, we expect you to share knowledge with each other, enjoy the opportunity to connect, and treat each other with the respect you wish to receive.
