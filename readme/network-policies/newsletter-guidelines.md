# Newsletter Guidelines

#### Purpose of this document

This document guides the Communications Committee in producing CSIDNet's monthly community update emails. These updates are one of the most visible expressions of the network's identity: they keep 600+ members across 49 countries informed, connected to opportunities, and cultivate a sense of belonging to this community. As the network grows, the goal is to make sure the Committee can run the full production cycle independently, with minimal reliance on staff.

From the [Communications Strategy](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1BffFx4irqc-_LZUnjF0m7sZGkuHWM07S/view?usp=sharing): Newsletter (public-facing): “Network Pulse” — the primary public email product, summarizing key updates, opportunities, and proof-of-work for members, partners, and the wider ecosystem.&#x20;

#### What is an update?

The CSIDNet Community Update is a monthly email sent to the full network listserv. It typically covers:

* Internal network news (governance, leadership, events, working groups, committee updates)
* Relevant external opportunities: grants, fellowships, calls for proposals
* Job postings in the CSID and adjacent fields
* Upcoming events across the ecosystem
* Resources including tools, datasets, publications, and recordings

#### Tone and voice

* Warm, collaborative, equity-centered
* Clear and accessible (avoid jargon; explain technical terms when used)
* Democratic, not hierarchical; “we” more than “I”
* Humble and transparent: share learnings from experiments, including what didn’t work, what we adjusted, and why we made those choices—not only successes.
* Reflective and experimental: communicate not just outcomes, but the reasoning behind approaches, trade-offs, and iterations.
* Urgent yet hopeful (climate-health realities + practical pathways)

#### Audience

The update reaches two overlapping audiences: CSIDNet members (researchers, practitioners, software developers actors across 49 countries) and public supporters including funders who have signed up to follow the network. Keep both in mind — internal updates should be accessible to external readers, and external opportunities should feel relevant, not generic.

#### Production cycle / Timeline

The Committee should aim to send the update in the first two weeks of each month, ideally by the 15th. Here is a recommended timeline:

25th of previous month: Send reminders to all committees, WGs, and staff to submit items. You can use the [content request form](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1v1ZAX6f3-cibhqyh1l9ogqMAATjtrUk9q3GDa4QsGQU/edit?usp=sharing).&#x20;

5th of month: Draft content with the submission, check for further relevant items.

10th of month: Internal review, double check and feedback.

13th of month: Final edits, formatting, test email to Comms Committee members if necessary.

15th of month: Send to the listserv.

### Content Structure&#x20;

**Subject line**: CSIDNet \[Month] Update: \[If possible, also an appealing key theme or news]\
&#xNAN;_&#x45;xamples:_

* CSIDNet Feb Update
* CSIDNet January Update: New Committees, Leadership Update, and Opportunities
* CSIDNet October Updates — Working Groups Confirmed

**Greeting**: We generally use “Dear CSIDNet Community” but anything else that refers to the whole listserv including members and non-members  is appropriate.&#x20;

**Opening paragraph**: This is the most personal part of the update. It should:

* Be written in first person (on behalf of the Communications Committee)
* Acknowledge the moment — the time of year, something happening in the world, or a community milestone
* Offer a brief emotional or thematic frame before moving to the news
* End with a transition into the body of the update
* This section should stay short — 2 to 4 sentences. Avoid generic phrases like "We hope this email finds you well."

**Network updates**: This section covers internal CSIDNet news. Gather inputs from the committees, WGs, and staff. Possible content includes:

* Leadership or governance changes
* Committee or working group milestones
* Event announcements or recaps (AGMs, regional convenings, training sessions)
* Membership updates or calls to action (e.g., elections, sign-ups)
* Fellow updates
* Any urgent reminders (deadlines, open calls)

Write each item as a short paragraph, not a bulleted list. Include relevant links. Credit people and committees by name where possible.

**Relevant News & Opportunities**: This section compiles external events, conferences, jobs, funding, fellowships, and calls for proposals that are relevant to CSIDNet members. For each item include:

* Name of the opportunity
* Deadline (if applicable)
* Geographic scope or eligibility note
* One-sentence description
* A link for details

Opportunities should be relevant to the community's work (climate, infectious disease, open science, research software, global health, etc.).

**Resources**: Share tools, datasets, publications, recordings, and other useful materials that have come across your radar. Include a one-sentence description for each. One or two items could be sufficient for this section.

#### Closing and sign-off

Close with a warm sign-off. Something simple like "With appreciation" or "Warmly" works well. Sign off with the names of the Communications Committee.&#x20;

#### How to collect content

* Add the [content submission form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSfL7ydgFIhCzqon5fXtsuuNIfZBA6RfV4be9_dvIZHbu4K7gQ/viewform?usp=dialog) at the bottom of your committee communications.
* Reach out to committees, working groups, fellow and individual members proactively to collect news and updates. You can use the [content request form](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1v1ZAX6f3-cibhqyh1l9ogqMAATjtrUk9q3GDa4QsGQU/edit?usp=sharing).&#x20;
* Stay alert in townhalls and training sessions for news, updates, and stories to follow up with people after the meeting and get details.
* Subscribe to newsletters relevant to the community to curate external content (DHIS2, Wellcome, rOpenSci, etc.).
* Join in other committees’ meetings to see how they are working, and create content to highlight their work either directly or through a blogpost you would write for the CSIDNet’s website.

#### Other recommendations

* Always verify that links work before including them.
* Rotate roles, so that the experience is built in the committee and workload is not on one or two people.
* Start with the internal news and move to external.&#x20;
* Start with the most important and urgent and move to other content.
* A test send would help identify mistakes and typos.&#x20;
* There is no word count, but aim for scannable length. Use clear identifiers and divisions so readers can find what matters to them. If the internal updates are strong, consider adding minimal or no external news.&#x20;
* Keep a running archive of all update emails in the Comms Committee Folder for archival purposes and organizational memory

#### Workflow and staff involvement in the process

* The Communications Committee leads the process and decides on the content.&#x20;
* The committee prepares the newsletter and sends it to staff to review within 24-48 hours.
* Staff feedback will likely be minimal. It can be editing the intro section, checking and tapping into opportunities for synergy they found in the content.
* Staff can provide internal content by adding it into [the newsletter content response sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1HYWZzghWsGH3ReKXK5cWsozZ0qS0k-E8UHkwySeVzYU/edit?gid=487058249#gid=487058249) by the deadline and factual accuracy review of the draft.&#x20;
* Prepare and save the newsletter draft in the Drive and give access to the staff.
